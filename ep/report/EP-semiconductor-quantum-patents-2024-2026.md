# Semiconductor Innovation Trends in European Patent Filings, Nov 2024 – Sep 2026

Nov 1, 2026 · @fini

## Executive summary

Between November 2024 and mid-September 2026, **8,062 non-PCT semiconductor applications** (7,560 families) were first published at the EPO and at national offices of EPO member states. Chip integration and packaging, not transistors, is the largest field: it appears in 51% of all applications.

**Most prominent technologies (absolute counts)**

- **Power module and discrete packaging: 1,127 applications.** Led by Infineon (160), Nexperia (69), Intel (63), Bosch (62) and Mitsubishi Electric (52). This is Europe's automotive power-electronics base showing up in patents.
- **2.5D, chiplets, interposers and RDL: 896.** Intel dominates (158), ahead of Samsung (61), Infineon (60), TSMC (54) and NXP (47).
- **Gate-all-around / nanosheet FETs: 683.** Almost entirely Intel (233), Samsung (176) and TSMC (145), with imec (39) and CEA (23) as the European research players.
- **3D chip stacking and TSV: 620.** Intel (153) and Samsung (81) lead; up 33% between comparable periods.
- **Quantum processor architecture and qubit control: 502**, plus 478 on quantum algorithms. Fujitsu, Infineon, Alice & Bob, eleQtron and Pasqal each have around 30 in the hardware group.
- **Power transistors are Europe's home ground:** power MOSFETs 423 (Infineon 118), IGBTs 245 (Infineon 75, Mitsubishi Electric 65), HEMTs 208 (STMicroelectronics 36, Samsung 29, Infineon 22).

**Most prominent players.** Samsung (720 applications), Intel (560), Infineon (508), TSMC (352), STMicroelectronics (298) and CEA (288) together appear on 2,726 applications, 34% of the corpus. Intel and Samsung lead in advanced logic and packaging. Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Bosch, Mitsubishi Electric and Nexperia lead in power devices. CEA is the largest research filer, with a profile spanning ReRAM, spin qubits and process technology.

**Momentum.** Total filings were flat (−5% between Nov 2024–Aug 2025 and Nov 2025–Aug 2026). The fastest-growing technologies are CFET stacked transistors (10 → 29), SRAM (24 → 46), glass and ceramic package substrates (75 → 117, driven by Intel and Absolics), quantum error correction (72 → 105) and 3D stacking (238 → 317). Front-end process categories such as lithography (−40%), doping (−36%) and deposition (−28%) declined.

Counts are full counts: one application can belong to several technologies, so categories do not add up to the total.

**Main caveat.** Excluding PCT-route filings removes several large players almost entirely: Qualcomm, IBM, Applied Materials, Huawei and Quantinuum file their European semiconductor applications mainly via PCT. The rankings show who files directly in Europe, not total European patent activity.

## Transistors and devices

Transistor filings split into two worlds with almost no overlap in applicants: advanced logic (GAA, FinFET, CFET) held by Intel, Samsung and TSMC, and power devices (power MOSFET, IGBT, HEMT, diodes) held by European and Japanese firms. The domain covers 2,906 applications and was stable (+2%).

**Data**

- **Advanced logic.** Gate-all-around is now larger than FinFET (683 vs 303 applications) and still growing (+18%); FinFET is flat (+4%). Intel, Samsung and TSMC hold 554 of the 683 GAA applications and 250 of the 303 FinFET applications. CFET, the next node after GAA, is still small (43) but almost tripled; TSMC (15) and imec (11) lead it.
- **Power devices.** Infineon leads power MOSFETs (118 of 423) and IGBTs (75 of 245), with Mitsubishi Electric second in IGBTs (65). In HEMTs (208), STMicroelectronics (36), Samsung (29), Infineon (22), CEA (17) and GlobalFoundries (14) are the leading filers. 186 of the 208 HEMT applications also carry the GaN/III-nitride material category, so HEMT here effectively means GaN HEMT. In power MOSFETs, 169 of 423 are SiC devices.
- **Display transistors.** TFTs (456) are a Korean field: LG (151) and Samsung (127) hold 61%, with BOE (41) third. Oxide-semiconductor (IGZO-type) channels grew 20%.
- **Beyond silicon.** 2D-material and carbon-nanotube FETs (39) and FeFETs (41) are small but growing; Samsung leads both.

**Filing route** (a pattern in the data): power-device filers use national offices heavily. Only 75 of 245 IGBT applications and 186 of 423 power-MOSFET applications are direct EP filings, against 411 of 683 for GAA. Bosch (2 of 247), Mitsubishi Electric (2 of 194) and Renesas (0 of 56) file almost exclusively at the German office, and so does TSMC (0 of 352).

**Interpretation.** Europe has no own player in leading-edge logic transistors; imec and CEA are the only European names among the top GAA filers, as research institutes. In power semiconductors Europe leads: Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Nexperia and Bosch are the main filers in every power category. The German-office route by TSMC and the Japanese power firms suggests that DE national filings, which become German patents faster and are cheaper, are used as the main European route in these fields.

## Memory

Memory (924 applications, +6%) is Samsung's field: it files 66% of all DRAM applications (166 of 251) and 46% of memory-assembly applications (115 of 251). Europe's role is in emerging, embedded memories.

**Data**

- **DRAM and HBM-type assemblies** are the two largest memory categories (251 each) and both are growing (+27% and +17%). SK hynix is a distant second in DRAM (20).
- **3D NAND** (233) is the only large memory category in decline (−8%). Samsung (82) leads, followed by YMTC (37), SK hynix (21) and Kioxia (18).
- **SRAM** nearly doubled (24 → 46). TSMC (27) and Intel (18) lead it; these filings sit next to their GAA logic work.
- **Emerging memories** are smaller and declining: ReRAM/PCM 169 (−19%), FeRAM 96 (−24%), MRAM 89 (−8%). CEA leads ReRAM/PCM (36) and is second in FeRAM (23); STMicroelectronics is third in ReRAM/PCM (22).

**Effect of the PCT exclusion.** Memory makers that enter Europe via PCT are underrepresented here. Among EP semiconductor publications in the same window, Micron has 36 Euro-PCT applications against 21 direct ones, and CXMT, a Chinese DRAM maker, 52 against 2. Samsung files almost entirely direct (719 of its 720 applications here are direct EP), which inflates its weight in every Samsung-heavy category. *Interpretation:* Samsung's DRAM share would be lower in a count that includes Euro-PCT.

## Chip integration, packaging and design

Integration is the largest domain (4,081 applications, 51% of the corpus) and has two separate halves: automotive power packaging led by European firms, and advanced logic packaging led by Intel, Samsung and TSMC. The domain declined 6% overall, but advanced packaging grew.

**Data**

- **Automotive and power packaging.** Thermal management (1,180), encapsulation (1,154) and power-module packaging (1,127) are the three largest categories in the whole study. Bosch (91), Infineon (89) and ZF (71) lead thermal management; ZF files 110 applications in total and Volkswagen 57, nearly all in packaging.
- **Advanced packaging.** 2.5D/chiplets/interposers (896) is Intel's strongest category (158). 3D stacking and TSV (620) grew fastest among the large categories (+33%). Hybrid bonding (413) is shared between Samsung (65), Intel (63), TSMC (43) and two French players, Soitec (32) and CEA (29).
- **Glass core substrates** are the fastest-growing packaging topic (75 → 117, +56%): Intel (87) and Absolics (38) account for most of it.
- **Backside power delivery** (167) is tied to GAA logic: 114 of these applications are also GAA filings. Samsung (67) and Intel (59) lead; the count fell 26%.
- **Monolithic 3D integration** grew 21% (261), with imec (29) as the leading non-Asian/non-US filer.
- **Chip design.** IC layout/EDA filings (G06F30/39, 150) fell 32%, while standard-cell and device-layout filings (184) rose 37%; Samsung, TSMC and Intel lead both. EDA tool vendors do not appear among the leaders; the only specialist in the top five is Arteris (8), an on-chip interconnect IP company.

**Interpretation.** Europe's integration strength lies in power and automotive packaging, where the main filers are European. In advanced logic packaging (chiplets, hybrid bonding, glass substrates), Europe's visible contributors are Soitec, CEA and imec, through wafer bonding and 3D-integration research. Fan-out packaging (15) is almost certainly undercounted: it has no own CPC group and is found by keyword only.

## Quantum technologies

**Data**

## Fabrication processes and equipment

Process and equipment filings (1,857) fell 19%, the steepest decline of any domain; eight of the nine process categories shrank. The field is led by equipment and materials suppliers rather than chipmakers.

**Data**

- **Equipment** (wafer handling and fab tools, 740) is led by Screen Holdings (42), ASML (39) and Kokusai Electric (37), with LPE (21), an Italian epitaxy-reactor maker, as the leading smaller European supplier.
- **Materials and wafers.** Shin-Etsu leads lithography and masks (50 of 129) and etching (39 of 292). Soitec leads wafer preparation (81 of 309) and is the fifth-largest filer in deposition (21).
- **Research.** CEA is the largest deposition filer (33) and appears in the top five of five process categories.

**Effect of the PCT exclusion.** Equipment and materials suppliers rely heavily on PCT. In the same window, Applied Materials has 131 Euro-PCT applications against 11 direct, Entegris 55 against 1, and NAURA 46 against 1. This domain is therefore the most incomplete in this study.

**Interpretation.** The decline may partly reflect classification timing: H10P only came into force with CPC 2026.01, and recently published documents may not yet carry their final H10P codes. A later re-run would show whether the drop holds.

**City level (e.g. Villach) is not in this dataset**, which records only the inventor's country. For the EP applications, a Villach count is therefore possible as a second step. ## Methodology and limitations

All counts come from the Google Patents Public Datasets table `patents-public-data.patents.publications`, queried on 1 Nov 2026 from BigQuery project `patent-study-513418`. Latest publication dates in the data: EP 16 Sep 2026, DE 24 Sep 2026, GB 16 Sep 2026, FR 4 Sep 2026.

**Selection**

1. Offices: EP plus the 39 EPO member states.
2. An application is counted once, at its first publication; it is in the window if that date is 1 Nov 2024 – 31 Oct 2026.
3. Excluded: anything with a PCT number (Euro-PCT and PCT national phase), national translations of EP patents (kind T1–T5), utility models (U), and A4 supplementary search reports.
4. Scope by CPC (any position, not only the first code): H10D, H10B, H10W, H10P, H10N60, G06N10, G06F30/39, B82Y10 (B82Y10 only together with a quantum keyword). Legacy H01L 21/23/25/27/29 was also searched, but no document in the window still carries it: CPC 2025.01 and 2026.01 moved devices to H10D and packaging and processes to the new H10W and H10P.
5. Out of scope: 501 applications that entered only through H10W/H10P and concern LEDs, solar cells or OLEDs (H10H, H10F, H10K, without any device or quantum code).
6. Duplicates: where an application had several publications on its first date, one record was kept. Families are DOCDB family IDs.

**Classification**

- 61 categories, each defined by CPC regular expressions and, where useful, keywords in title and abstract (English, German, French). Keywords are only applied to applications already in the matching CPC domain. All rules are stored in the table `patent_study.rules`.
- Every assignment records its basis: CPC, CPC+text, or text only. Of 18,371 assignments, 16,270 rest on CPC alone, 1,402 on CPC confirmed by text and 699 on text only (96% CPC-based); seven categories are text-only (CFET, fan-out, trapped ion, neutral atom, photonic, topological, Ga2O3).
- Full counting: an application is counted in every category it matches. 334 applications fall into a domain but no specific category (e.g. only electrode or connector codes).
- Spot check: a sample of text-based and CPC-based assignments was read. One error was found and fixed: H10D30/47 also covers nanoribbon FETs, so HEMT was restricted to H10D30/471 ff. and H10D30/015.

**Applicants**

- Starting point is Google's harmonised assignee name. Name variants and subsidiaries were merged into 51 groups, e.g. all Infineon entities, Samsung Electronics + Samsung Display + Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Display + LG Electronics + LG Innotek, BOE and its subsidiaries, Kia + Hyundai Motor + Hyundai Mobis, the two CEA name variants. Co-applicants each receive full credit.
- HQ countries in the top-20 table are the group's headquarters; the origin figures in Scope use the first-named applicant's country.
- Applicant type is rule-based (company / university or research / other); 138 applications by unsuffixed names could not be classified.

**Known limitations**

- **PCT exclusion biases the picture.** In the same window, Qualcomm (188), Huawei (140), IBM (138), Applied Materials (131), BOE (83), Google (74), Microsoft (71), CXMT (52) and Quantinuum (50) filed semiconductor applications at the EPO mainly or only via PCT. Their weight here is far below their real European activity.
- **National coverage is uneven.** Italian data ends in Dec 2025, British data thins out sharply in 2026, and small offices are sparsely covered.
- **Text-only categories** depend on keywords and probably undercount, especially for national filings in other languages.
- **Publication, not filing.** The window is by first publication, which lags filing by about 18 months; the trends describe inventions filed roughly in 2023–2025.

**Data and cost**

- BigQuery processed about 610 GB in total (two corpus builds of 263–264 GB each; the first build missed H10W/H10P and was redone), within the free 1 TB/month.
- Tables in `patent-study-513418.patent_study`: `corpus` (raw selection), `apps_final` (8,062 applications in scope), `rules`, `cats` (application × category with basis), `appl` (applicant grouping), `application_list` (the full list, one row per application).
- **Export before about 31 Dec 2026**: tables in a BigQuery sandbox expire 60 days after creation. In the BigQuery console, query `SELECT * FROM patent_study.application_list` and use Save results → CSV (Google Drive).

The final selection query:

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE `patent-study-513418.patent_study.corpus` AS
WITH first_pub AS (
  SELECT application_number, MIN(publication_date) AS first_publication_date
  FROM `patents-public-data.patents.publications`
  WHERE country_code IN ('EP','AL','AT','BE','BG','CH','CY','CZ','DE','DK','EE','ES','FI','FR','GB','GR','HR','HU','IE','IS','IT','LI','LT','LU','LV','MC','ME','MK','MT','NL','NO','PL','PT','RO','RS','SE','SI','SK','SM','TR')
  GROUP BY application_number
)
SELECT p.publication_number, p.country_code, p.kind_code, p.application_number,
  p.family_id, p.publication_date, f.first_publication_date, p.filing_date, p.priority_date,
  (SELECT t.text FROM UNNEST(p.title_localized) t ORDER BY t.language = 'en' DESC LIMIT 1) AS title,
  (SELECT a.text FROM UNNEST(p.abstract_localized) a ORDER BY a.language = 'en' DESC LIMIT 1) AS abstract,
  ARRAY(SELECT c.code FROM UNNEST(p.cpc) c) AS cpc_all,
  ARRAY(SELECT c.code FROM UNNEST(p.cpc) c WHERE c.first) AS cpc_first,
  ARRAY(SELECT c.code FROM UNNEST(p.cpc) c WHERE c.inventive) AS cpc_inventive,
  ARRAY(SELECT a.name FROM UNNEST(p.assignee_harmonized) a) AS applicants,
  ARRAY(SELECT a.country_code FROM UNNEST(p.assignee_harmonized) a) AS applicant_countries
FROM `patents-public-data.patents.publications` p
JOIN first_pub f USING (application_number)
WHERE f.first_publication_date BETWEEN 20241101 AND 20261031
  AND p.publication_date = f.first_publication_date
  AND (p.pct_number IS NULL OR p.pct_number = '')
  AND p.kind_code NOT IN ('T','T1','T2','T3','T4','T5','U','U1','U8','A4')
  AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM UNNEST(p.cpc) c WHERE REGEXP_CONTAINS(c.code,
    r'^(H10D|H10B|H10W|H10P|H10N60/|H01L(21|23|25|27|29)/|G06N10/|B82Y10/|G06F30/39)'))
```
